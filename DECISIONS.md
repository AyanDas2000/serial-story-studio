# DECISIONS
Serial Story Studio | Ayan Das | 8 October 2026

**Episode 150 context.** Current v1 sends the adopted premise/spine, plan purpose, current episode's job, voice/style instructions and all earlier prose. It does not retrieve facts or feed the extracted Memory ledger back into generation. This favors simplicity and exact history on the demonstrated short slice, but is not a proven 200-episode memory architecture. At scale I would add arc summaries, a rolling recent window and selected evidence-backed character/fact/open-thread records, with provenance and invalidation when an earlier source changes.

**Human control.** Saving and adopting direction/plan are separate. The author edits drafts, chooses rewrites, approves the exact final prose and confirms extracted facts. Chat advises but cannot adopt, approve or save memory. Gates exist where a decision changes governing direction, canonical text or recorded claims. Approval locks canon; retroactive canon editing is not implemented. Editing an earlier unapproved draft flags later episodes for review, without silently rewriting them.

**Consistency checks.** Quotes must anchor to the accepted prose; stale edits and stale draft results are rejected or set aside. Secret trigger words are checked before requests; new capitalized names are surfaced after drafting. These are narrow mechanical checks, not a semantic continuity or repetition critic. They cannot guarantee entailment, detect every paraphrased secret or catch unsupported narrative facts; an independent literary review of the prior live run requested revisions.

**First failure at scale.** Full-history context grows linearly per episode and repeated work grows roughly quadratically over the serial. Attention, latency and cost degrade; the long fact list and flat plan also become hard to review. Better bounded retrieval and summaries need evaluation against missed facts, stale summaries, reveal windows and unresolved threads. Approximate measured prior cost was $0.04–$0.07 per draft; naive 200-draft extrapolation is $8–$14 before retries, edits, extraction, context growth and gateway fees, so it is not a valid upper bound. No 200-episode latency measurement exists. Provider calls reserve worst-case spend before sending; uncertain outcomes are not automatically retried. The local cap is not a provider billing guarantee.
