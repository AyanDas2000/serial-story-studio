# Episode 2: Two Sides of the Door

Draft, not approved.

Ron began with the door. He knelt at the frame with Maya's flashlight and went over the inside face of it slowly. The bolt was a heavy barrel bolt, black iron, set into a keeper on the jamb. The keeper had split away from the wood where the pry bar had gone in, and the screws hung out of the splinters. Everywhere else the gray film lay on the iron like frost.

"Three ways to seal a room," he said. "A hand on the bolt from inside. A trick from outside that throws it through a gap. Or the room was never sealed, and somebody found a way out that nobody has thought of."

"You've decided to say it out loud, then."

"It helps me to hear which one embarrasses me." He ran the light along the edge of the door. "The trick first. A string, a wire, a magnet, whatever it is, it needs a gap. Do you have a sheet of paper?"

Maya tore a page from her notebook. Ron tried to slide it between door and jamb at the height of the bolt, then lower, then along the sill. The weatherstrip was continuous rubber, thick and old, and the paper would not enter anywhere. He turned the light on the strip itself. Under the gray bloom, a line of undisturbed dust ran along its whole length.

"Nothing has been drawn through here in a long time," he said. "And the film on the bolt is whole, except where the maintenance man's pry bar broke it. A bolt thrown by a thread would have a scuffed barrel. This one doesn't. That's one gone."

"And the other exit?"

"You told me the vents are fixed panes. I'd like to see that for myself." He fetched the stepladder from beside the potting bench. He went along the roof glass pane by pane, and Maya held the ladder. Every pane was glazed into the frame with old putty, and the putty was cracked but unbroken, with the same film on it. The vents were screwed shut, and the screw heads were rusted into their slots. He came down with gray on his fingers.

"Nobody has opened a pane in this room since before whatever killed the plants," he said. "So that's two."

"Which leaves a hand on the bolt from inside."

"Which leaves the one I like least, because it forces me to choose between a man who bolted himself in, a man who bolted someone else in, and a man who says he found it bolted." He wiped his hands on a rag. "I'm not ready to choose."

Footsteps rang on the service stairs. Moss lifted his head on the gravel outside and lowered it again. Arjun Sethi came through the door first, in his good coat, a folder under one arm and wind in his hair. Behind him came a man Ron did not know, heavy in the shoulders, with the face of someone who has been told bad news twice and believed it once.

"Ron," Arjun said. "Sergeant." He nodded to Maya with the care of a man stepping around a puddle. "This is my client. He owns the greenhouse, and the service contract, and the plants."

The owner looked down the aisle at the brown heaps and said nothing. Then he said, "Thirty years. Some of those were older than the building's lease."

"He'd like to retain you," Arjun said to Ron. "To find out what happened to the room. Privately. Under my direction, so that what you learn is protected."

Maya's jaw moved. "It's my scene, Arjun."

"I'm not disputing that. I'm asking that my client be allowed to know what's in his own greenhouse."

"A man died in it."

"And my client is not the man who died. He's here voluntarily, and he'll answer reasonable questions with me beside him." Arjun said it gently, which made it harder. "That's all."

Ron saw the owner's eyes travel the length of the room and stop at the far bench. They stayed on the orchid. His face did not change, but he took a long time to look away.

"Ron," Maya said quietly. "If you take his money, what you find isn't mine anymore."

"If I don't, I'm a man with a hunch and a pail of putty on his hands." Ron looked from her to Arjun, and for a moment he was the only one in the room who did not look troubled. "I'll take the job. One condition. Anything I find that touches that bolt or that man on the floor goes to Maya the same hour it goes to you."

Arjun opened his mouth, closed it, and looked at his client. The owner was still looking at the orchid.

"That's not a condition I can promise," Arjun said.

"Then ask him," said Ron.
