# Episode 1

Maya Brandt met him at the top of the service stairs, where the stairwell opened onto a gravel roof and a wind that smelled of tar. Beyond her stood the greenhouse, a long glass room with a pale sky behind it. Moss came up the last step at Ron's knee, and Ron touched the dog's head once. Moss sat down on the gravel and waited.

"You said it was strange," Ron said.

"I said come and look." Maya held the door and let him go first. "Night watchman for the building. The morning maintenance man found him on the floor around six. The door was bolted on the inside. He had to get a pry bar from the stairwell to get in, and the frame is split where he did it. That's the only way in. The vents are fixed panes."

The air in the greenhouse was warm and stale. Ron stood still and let it come to him. Along both walls, on benches and in hanging baskets, every plant had collapsed. Ferns lay in brown heaps, and the leaves of something broad had gone to paper. Nothing was torn or trampled. It looked as if the room had been drained all at once.

"Who owns it?" he asked.

"The building's tenants share it. Someone pays a service to keep it up. I've got an officer getting the name." Maya nodded at the floor, where chalk marked a place near the middle aisle. "He was there. The body's gone to the examiner, and nobody's told me a cause. No wound that we could see. Patrol thinks he locked himself in for some reason and had a medical event."

"A man bolts himself into a room full of dead plants and then falls down."

"I know how it sounds."

"No, it's a fine theory. It just starts with the door." Ron walked slowly down the aisle with his hands behind him. "Patrol sees a locked room and asks how a man died in it. I'd ask whether he was meant to be found in it. Whether the room was the point."

"Meaning what?"

"I don't know yet. A room can be a container, or a message, or an alibi." He stopped at a bench of black, slumped stems. "Say the bolt was thrown by the watchman because he was afraid. Then something was already wrong when he came up here. Say it was thrown by someone else, and the way out was through the door he later pried. Say neither, and the bolt is a story somebody wanted told."

"Those are three different crimes."

"They're three different rooms. Only one of them is this one."

Maya let out a breath that was almost a laugh. "This is why I called you instead of writing it up."

"What did he carry?"

"Keys, a flashlight, a thermos. The thermos was still half full and I've bagged it. Nothing else on him." She watched him. "Ron, look at the end bench."

He had already seen it. At the far end, under the last pane, sat a single orchid, its stem arched and its pale flowers open and unmarked. Around it the room had died and the orchid had not.

He went to it and crouched. He didn't touch it. The pot was clay, ordinary, with a paper tag pushed into the soil and its writing washed out. The leaves were firm. The bench around it was filmed with a gray residue, a dried bloom like salt, which lay over the wood, the neighboring pots and the fallen leaves alike.

Except in one place. The orchid sat in a clean circle, the wood bare and darker, exactly the width of its pot.

"Maya. Come here and tell me what you see."

She bent beside him. "A plant that made it."

"Look at the bench."

She looked a long moment. "The film's everywhere but under the pot."

"The film is on everything that was here while it settled. Under a pot, it wouldn't land. But look at the edge of the ring, here. It's sharp. If this pot had stood here while the others died, the film would have crept in around it, and the ring would be soft." He sat back on his heels. "I think this orchid wasn't here when the room began to go. I think someone carried it in afterward."

"Or it was here and survived, and you're reading a lot into a smudge."

"I might be." He said it easily, as he always did, and then he said, "But if I'm right, it matters who wanted one living thing in a dead room. And it matters why."

Maya was quiet. Outside, the wind worked at the panes, and by the door Moss had not moved.

"Don't let anyone touch that pot," Ron said. "Not the tag, not the saucer. And find me whoever pays the service. I want to know who last brought a plant up here."
