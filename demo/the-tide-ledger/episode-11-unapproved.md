# The Tide Ledger, episode 11

Unapproved draft.

The widow's neighbour on the second landing was on Iona's slips. Leda Moss, Seven Cooper's Stair, and an age that had been twenty-two when Iona copied it. Neri had found the door while Sef rested on the thirty-third step. She nodded at it and stayed where she could watch the stairs.

Leda Moss opened it with a baby on her hip and a wet cloth over her shoulder. Her hands were cracked red across the knuckles, the hands of someone who took in washing. She heard Iona's name, then the words *Flood Office*, and began to close the door.

"I'm not here from the office," Iona said. "I work in it. Your name is in a ledger in its cellar, under a heading that says you were never born. I came to ask if that's anything you know."

The door stopped. "Never born," Leda said. "That's tidier than what they told me."

She let them into a room with one window, where the washing hung in rows. She put the child on a blanket and did not offer chairs. She stood with her arms crossed, as a person does who has told this story to counters and not been believed.

"I was twenty when the Surge came. After, I went to the Dock Hiring Hall for a loading card, like everyone. You give your name, they ask for the birth entry, they send a runner to the Flood Office to look it up." She said it flatly. "He came back and said there was nothing. I said there'd been a parish book. They said the parish book was cancelled. I said by whom, and they said I'd have to ask the Flood Office, and the Flood Office said apply to the Hill register, and the Hill said I wasn't on it."

"So no card," Iona said.

"No card. No card, no berth on the quays. The ferries wanted the card, and so did the laundries with a licence, and the fish sheds. Even the Hill school wanted an entry before it would take my sister's girl, who was born that same winter on the Hill, and was entered fine." She laughed shortly. "I didn't need to be a person. I needed to be a person with a paper."

"What did you do?"

"What anyone does. Worked for people who didn't ask. The Stair's full of them, if you look. Scrubbing, gutting, hauling on the tide shifts, cash in hand and no wage book, so no one could say I'd been there. When a man short-paid me, I had no one to tell. If I'd gone to the watch, they'd have asked who I was." She shifted her arms. "I've no claim to the room either. The landlord writes a rent book in his own name."

Neri, at the door, had her field book out, writing small. Leda glanced at it and let it be.

"Have you anything from then?" Iona asked. "Anything with a date on it?"

Leda hesitated. She crossed to a cracked teapot on the shelf and drew out a folded square of buff card, soft as cloth from handling. "They gave this to me at the hall. So I'd stop coming back."

It was a printed refusal slip, half the size of a hand. The Dock Hiring Hall's name ran across the top in black. Under it, in a clerk's pen, the name *L. Moss*, and a line that read *No entry of birth: F.O. search, returned negative*, with a date in the first spring after the Surge. In the corner was a number written in red crayon, and below it a smudged stamp.

Iona held it by the edges. She knew the words *F.O. search*. She had written them herself, in the office's own abbreviation, on a hundred answers to a hundred runners.

"They sent a runner to the Flood Office," she said slowly. "Every time. And someone wrote the answer, and kept a copy."

"I wouldn't know."

"I do." The Flood Office kept a search book at the front counter, a cheap ruled ledger replaced yearly. Each query was entered, with the name asked after and the clerk's initials, and it was the one record that nobody on the lower floor bothered to box, because it was thought to be only counter work. If it had survived, it held every time Leda Moss had been refused, and every other name on her slips. It would show whose initials had written *negative* on people who had once been alive on the Hill's own lists.

"May I copy this?" she asked. "I'll bring it back whole."

"Copy it, but don't take it." Leda took the slip back with her fingertips, as though it might yet be confiscated. "You said it was in a ledger in a cellar. I wasn't a page in a ledger, I was a woman waiting on a bench. What are you going to do about the bench?"

"Find who sat behind the counter."

Outside, Neri traced the slip on the landing. Sef stood and looked at Iona's face, and said nothing.

"The search books are upstairs," Iona told them. "They aren't in the vault. I had them listed for the skip last week."

Neri's pencil stopped. "Then they're gone?"

"They go Thursday morning, before the grout." She was already on the stairs. "I'm going back this afternoon, before the sexton. Tonight, if I have to."
